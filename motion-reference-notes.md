# Brand story reference notes

User-selected direction: DIA Studio (dia.tv), especially its Nike, Squarespace and Coinbase examples, and Lusion (lusion.co). Large typography, motion as identity, a persistent graphic language, restrained layout and purposeful transformations are the brief.

Current-run research: Lusion's public HTML returned HTTP 200; its published script contains WebGL rendering, animation-frame scheduling, interpolation, pointer and scroll handling. DIA's root, work and Coinbase paths returned HTTP 406, including a browser-like user agent; its robots response was also 406. A public Squarespace blog path was 404 and a search response contained a redirect/challenge rather than usable case-study results. Those responses are not presented as fresh visual verification of DIA's projects. The user's named examples remain art-direction references; no claim is made to reproduce their internal implementation.

Our translation is one material and four strands throughout, topology-preserving morphs, arc-length sampling, phase-aligned closed curves, quintic interpolation and cubic-spline drawing. The opening N–R–T–H is drawn live from the first frame. Industry symbols express care, movement, systems, composition, gathering and discovery. Native scrolling and reversible state retain visitor control. Supporting original sculpture media was generated with ImageGen; Higgsfield has no callable tools in this session. No reference art or code is copied.

## Accessible DIA case-study research

A subsequent public search on It's Nice That located [DIA's Onchain Summer identity for Coinbase/Base](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/dia-studio-onchain-summer-graphic-design-project-190224), published February 19, 2024. The article describes circles derived from the Base logo, chainlinks expressing interaction, a generative animation tool and a strict typographic system adapted to aspect ratios. Two published animation sequences were downloaded for reference inspection only: the 40-frame circle/typography sequence (03) and the 107-frame deforming beaded-letter sequence (05). Frames at the start, one-third and two-thirds were inspected in this run; evidence is in artifacts/brand-story/references. They substantiate a reusable circle motif and human-feeling deformation of a consistent logotype. This research informed NRTH's persistent strands and shared ring language; no frames are shipped with the site. Nike and Squarespace examples have not been freshly visually verified here.
